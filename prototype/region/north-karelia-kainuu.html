North Karelia and Kainuu

FinlandEastern Finnish lakeland and taiga: Karelian and Kainuu uplands

Boreal forest, lake and bog country in eastern Finland; 15 of 18 municipalities have shrunk since 1990. No national arrival programme was found, so belonging starts with Finnish and a village association. The Sámi homeland lies outside it.

Land standing · qualitative, never scored

Finnish village communities and private forest owners; in the east the ground carries Karelian evacuee and Orthodox history (New Valamo moved to Heinävesi from Valaam in 1940), and in Kuhmo Kalevala-rooted culture. Not the Sámi homeland or the reindeer herding area.

How to arrive in North Karelia and Kainuu

Where arriving in a place would harm the community already there, the honest answer is not to go.

Every region in this tool is already someone’s home.

What living here asks of you

North Karelia and Kainuu ask you to arrive as a neighbour in a place that is emptying, not as its rescuer. The footprint holds about 219,000 people on 29,800 square kilometres, 7.4 per square kilometre and 4.1 outside Joensuu and Kajaani, after losing 13 percent of its people since 1990, and 38 percent leaving out Joensuu, Kajaani, Kontiolahti and Liperi. The ground is forest, lake and bog, with peatland on 32 percent of North Karelia's forestry land and 45 percent of Kainuu's (county-wide figures), so build light and read municipal plans first: Ilomantsi is preparing a partial master plan to enable gold mining along its gold-bearing belt. Snow lies deepest in mid-March, usually 50 to 70 centimetres in North Karelia and more on the uplands. No national arrival programme was found; the doors are village associations, Leader groups and municipal moving services, and Finnish is what opens them. For the Sámi homeland and the reindeer herding municipalities of northern Kainuu, the answer is not there.

The way in · qualitative · not legal advice

What lawful entry involves, in order

Who may hold
Citizens of an EU or EEA state, and entities seated in the EU or EEA, buy land without a permit, unless a non-EU/EEA person holds at least one tenth of such an entity. Anyone else needs a Ministry of Defence permit for each property. The permit is not granted if the purchase can be assessed to threaten national security or to hinder defence, territorial surveillance, border control or security of supply. Since 15 July 2025 the Ministry can prevent acquisitions by people and entities whose state is waging a war of aggression, and it names Russia and Belarus among them. Act 470/2019 on Permit Requirements for Certain Real Estate Acquisitions, ss.1, 2 and 5 (Finlex); Ministry of Defence, Authorisation to non-EU and non-EEA buyers
How a group can hold
Yhteismetsä (jointly owned forest)Osakaskunta (joint-area association)
  • Yhteismetsä (jointly owned forest). Owners of properties can found a jointly owned forest by agreement. The shareholders' association is a legal person that can hold rights and make commitments, and shareholders are not personally liable for its obligations. The forest is meant for sustainable forestry for its shareholders, so it suits shared woodland rather than a settlement. Act on Commonly Owned Forests 109/2003 ss.1, 4 and 5
  • Osakaskunta (joint-area association). A common area owned jointly by two or more properties is held by an association of the property owners, which the Joint Areas Act sets out how to run. It is for shared land and water areas, such as shores and fishing rights, attached to existing properties. Joint Areas Act 758/1989 ss.2 and 3
First claim
  • The municipality holds a pre-emption right on property sales in its area, used to acquire land for community building, recreation and conservation. It does not apply to properties of 5,000 square metres or less, or where the buyer is the seller's spouse or an heir (Pre-emption Act 608/1977 ss.1 and 5).
  • The state holds a pre-emption right where acquisition is needed for defence, border surveillance or national security, on properties in or within 1,000 metres of areas reserved for the Defence Forces or the Border Guard and of listed installations (Act 469/2019 ss.1 and 2).
  • Staying in the border zone, which can reach up to 3 kilometres from the border on land, needs a Border Guard permit; it is granted open-ended to someone who lives in the zone or holds a dwelling or property there (Border Guard Act 578/2005 ss.49, 52 and 53).
Direction
The rules have tightened since 2022: the state's pre-emption right was extended (Act 469/2019 as amended in 2022), the 2025 amendment lets the Ministry of Defence prevent acquisitions by nationals of states waging a war of aggression (in force 15 July 2025), and the Building Act 751/2023 took effect on 1 January 2025. EU and EEA buyers are not affected by the permit rules.tightening · as of 2026

What the law rules out
  • Everyman's rights allow moving in nature and staying or overnighting only temporarily, irrespective of ownership; they exclude disturbing the landowner's use of the land, entering yards and cultivated fields, felling trees, lighting fires on another's land, and camping or making noise too close to dwellings, so they are not a way to settle (Ministry of the Environment, Jokaisenoikeudet).
  • In the reindeer herding area reindeer herding may be practised irrespective of land ownership (Reindeer Husbandry Act 848/1990 s.3), so ownership of a parcel there carries no right to exclude it.
  • A non-EU/EEA buyer cannot hold a property after a final refusal of the permit: it must be sold on within six months, failing which the authorities arrange the sale (Act 470/2019 s.6).

  • The border-zone width is set by decree, which was not opened; only the statutory maximum of three kilometres on land is stated.
  • Municipal building orders and which shores are covered by a shore plan were not checked for any municipality in the footprint.
  • No source states how long the Ministry of Defence takes to decide a permit.
  • No statutory arrival programme and no group-friendly host-side offer was found; the village associations, Leader groups and Joensuu's moving agent are pages that exist, and what each offers a group was not opened.

Where legal and cost questions come first

These are the questions a community has to be able to answer in a place: who may hold the land, how a group can hold it together, and what it costs. Read them as context for a conversation with the people already there, not as a verdict on the place.

Legal and ownership

Foreign ownership
restricted EU and EEA citizens, and entities seated in the EU/EEA without 10% or more of votes held by a non-EU/EEA person, buy freehold without a permit. Non-EU/EEA individuals and entities need a Ministry of Defence permit under the Act on Permit Requirements for Certain Real Estate Acquisitions (470/2019); no permit is needed when the seller is a spouse or someone the buyer could inherit from, or when spouses buy together and one does not need a permit. Since 15 July 2025 the Ministry can prevent acquisitions by nationals and entities of states waging a war of aggression; the Ministry names Russia and Belarus among the states that meet the criteria (decree in force 15 July 2025). Permanent residence permits are taken into account.
Collective ownership path
Jointly owned forest (yhteismetsä, Act 109/2003: property owners form a shareholders' association for sustainable forestry, shareholders not personally liable); joint-area associations (osakaskunta) for shared land and water areas (Joint Areas Act 758/1989). Co-operatives and housing companies are used locally (Auroora in Rääkkylä is a Kotocoop co-operative housing unit); their statutes were not opened.
Several households on one holding
with conditions
Residency needed to buy
no residency required
Where building is decided
Building permit from the municipality for any new dwelling or building of 30 m2 or more (Building Act 751/2023 ss.42, 61; in force 1 Jan 2025). On a lake or river shore no new building without a detailed plan or a master plan that expressly allows permits (Land Use Act 132/1999 s.72), with exceptions for farm, forestry and fishing buildings and small additions; planning-need areas (s.16) add a further test.
First-claim holders
Municipality: pre-emption right on property sales in the municipality (Pre-emption Act 608/1977 s.1); none for properties of 5,000 m2 or less, sales to a spouse or heir, or forced auctions (s.5); State: pre-emption right where acquisition is needed for defence, border surveillance or national security, within 1,000 m of defence and Border Guard areas and facilities (Act 469/2019 ss.1-2)
Key restriction
Non-EU/EEA buyers need a Ministry of Defence permit, which can be refused to Russian and Belarusian buyers; the state may pre-empt near defence and Border Guard sites; shore building needs a plan; staying inside the border zone (up to 3 km from Russia) needs a Border Guard permit, open-ended for residents and property-holders (Border Guard Act 578/2005 ss.49-53).
Legal direction
tightening Tightening in 2022-2025: state pre-emption right extended (Act 469/2019, amended 1099/2022), the 2025 amendment letting the Ministry block acquisitions by nationals of aggressor states (in force 15 July 2025), and the 2025 Building Act reform (751/2023, in force 1 Jan 2025). EU/EEA buyers are unaffected.

Field reality, water and soil

Water source control

Rights regime
Water Act 587/2011: water in wells and tanks belongs to the owner of the well; other open water and groundwater are controlled by the owner of the water or land area within the Act's limits (ch.2 s.1); groundwater abstraction of more than 250 m3 per day, and water-economy projects generally, need a permit (ch.3-4). Shared water areas are managed by joint-area associations (osakaskunta, Act 758/1989).
Rights holder
mixed
Single-entity control
low Water stress is very low in every basin (Aqueduct bau50_ws_x_r 0.0001 to 0.033). Lake and shore water is held by many private owners and joint-area associations. One large industrial operation in the footprint is the Sotkamo mine (Oulujoki basin); its environmental record was not checked here and is a due-diligence item, not a finding about village water.
When water is short
No prior-appropriation system found: abstraction is governed by the owner's right of use plus a permit above the 250 m3/day threshold; allocation rules for drought were not found.

Source: Water Act 587/2011 (Finlex); Joint Areas Act 758/1989 (Finlex); WRI Aqueduct 4.0 (opens in a new tab) · medium confidence

Known gaps. Municipal water-supply utilities, water co-operatives and well-water quality were not researched; the 250 m3/day threshold was read from the project list in the Act, not from the specific section on groundwater.

Known contamination
legacy mining Sotkamo hosts the Talvivaara-Terrafame nickel-zinc mine (opened 1 October 2008 by Talvivaara; Talvivaara went bankrupt and Terrafame acquired the site in 2015, per Terrafame's own history page). Its environmental record was not checked against a regulator source. Outokumpu is the site of a former copper mine (the municipality is named after it). Ilomantsi has a gold-mining zone under municipal planning (Southern Gold Line partial master plan).
Contamination register
not read
Due-diligence burden
moderate
Regulatory regime
Environmental Protection Act 527/2014 ch.14: the polluter must clean; if the polluter cannot be found or does not, the area holder who consented to the pollution or knew or should have known of the area's state when acquiring it must clean it, unless obviously unreasonable; otherwise the municipality (s.133). Assessment reports go to the Permit and Supervision Agency (s.135). Soil condition data sit in the national MATTI information system (about 29,000 areas recorded, over 7,000 remediation decisions).

Source: Finlex 527/2014 s.133-135; Terrafame history page (terrafame.com); Municipality of Ilomantsi (opens in a new tab) · low confidence

Known gaps. The MATTI register itself and the SYKE/ELY records for Sotkamo and Outokumpu were not opened; the mine's contamination and leak history was not verified against a regulator page and is deliberately not asserted here; the Outokumpu mine fact is from a secondary source; whether MATTI entries are public per site was not verified.

Climate buffering, state and trajectory

Buffering features (state)

Primary features
lake effect forest canopy peat water storage
Altitude range
72-296 (Global Solar Atlas elevation at 318 sample points; summits and fells are higher)
Buffering strength
moderate
Detail
FMI describes North Karelia as a continental province split between a thermally favourable lake region (Höytiäinen, Pyhäselkä, Pielinen valley) and barren watershed uplands, and Kainuu as typically continental: annual mean 1991-2020 about +2 to +4 C in North Karelia, large seasonal swings, harsh frosts and heat spells under Russian high pressure. Lakes and the peat that covers 32 to 45 percent of forestry land store water and moderate local temperature.

Trajectory under warming

Direction
buffering falling FMI (CMIP5 RCP scenarios, context only): warming of about 1.8-5.6 C in North Karelia and 1.9-5.8 C in Kainuu this century against 1981-2010, largest from November to March; precipitation +6 to +16% (North Karelia) and +6 to +17% (Kainuu); 1991-2020 already about 0.6 C warmer than 1981-2010. Snow depth in mid-March is 50-70 cm now (70-80 cm on the uplands). WorldClim SSP2-4.5 2041-2060 footprint mean is 5.4 C.
Primary vulnerability
shorter snow season and milder, wetter winters

Source: Finnish Meteorological Institute ilmasto-opas: Pohjois-Karjala and Kainuu regional climate articles (updated 2022); WorldClim CMIP6 v2.1 (opens in a new tab) · medium confidence

Known gaps. Spruce bark-beetle and forest-damage stress named in pre-research was not verified; FMI scenarios are CMIP5-based and cannot be relabelled SSP2-4.5.

Who made this

The framework was originated by Askja and is developed by a small working group of practitioners and researchers. The demonstration site was built by one member of that group, who holds the practitioner seat.

Members are named by first name and role only, as in the working documents; no surnames, handles or contact details appear anywhere on the site.

There is no public contact route yet; the working group will set one.

How to read it

  • Thresholds filter; they never score or rank.
  • Land standing is qualitative and never a filter.
  • Every value carries its source and vintage.
  • The rivers and contours drawn on this page are ornament, not surveyed. For geography, use the map and ask the people who live there.
