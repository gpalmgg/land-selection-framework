Teruel uplands and the Maestrazgo (Iberian System)

SpainIberian System uplands: Albarracín, Gúdar–Javalambre and the Maestrazgo

Cold, water-short Iberian uplands where the Tagus, the Turia and tributaries of the Ebro rise, and 85 of 180 municipalities have fewer than a hundred people. The way in is a town hall's or a working farmer's invitation and a rented house first, then a long look at the water and the fire.

Land standing · qualitative, never scored

Small Aragonese upland villages and the commons that bind some of them. In the Sierra de Albarracín the historic Comunidad de Albarracín still exists, a recognised local entity whose 2007 statutes list 23 municipalities, whose mayors meet in a plega, and which holds forest montes in common with the city of Albarracín; the Comunidad de Aldeas de Teruel did not survive the 1830s abolition

Where the answer is not here

  • Building a new multi-household settlement on non-urban land: a single home there needs a parcel of at least 10,000 square metres where no population nucleus can form, and two or more homes within 150 metres count as one.
  • Taking a scarce house in a village whose municipality names the lack of rural housing as its problem.

How to arrive in Teruel uplands and the Maestrazgo (Iberian System)

Where arriving in a place would harm the community already there, the honest answer is not to go.

Every region in this tool is already someone’s home. The river stops here by choice.

What living here asks of you

The Teruel uplands ask you to arrive by invitation, not by purchase. On non-urban land one house needs a parcel of at least 10,000 square metres in a place where no cluster of homes forms, so the way in is a municipality's own housing or work offer, rent first, or the rehabilitation of an old village house or deserted aldea, which the law lets you divide if its traditional shape is kept. Pueblos Vivos Aragón helps, but serves only EU nationals and people already holding Spanish work and residence permits, and gives no money. Rural housing is scarce enough, a network workshop says, to hold back local young people too. These are headwaters: the Tagus rises near Frías de Albarracín, and a Cuencas Mineras mayor wrote in September 2026 of a summer of fire and violent storm. Wind and solar projects are contested. Some forest montes of the Sierra de Albarracín belong to the Comunidad de Albarracín, a neighbour with its own rules.

Source: Decreto Legislativo 1/2014, Ley de Urbanismo de Aragón, arts. 34-35 (texto refundido, BOE consolidated) (opens in a new tab)

The way in · qualitative · not legal advice

What lawful entry involves, in order

Who may hold
Foreigners enjoy the same civil rights as Spaniards in Spain except where special laws or treaties say otherwise (Civil Code art. 27). The special regime is a set of restricted-access zones for foreign buyers: islands, Cartagena, the Strait of Gibraltar, the bay of Cadiz, the Portuguese and French frontier zones, Galicia and the Spanish territories in North Africa, with percentage caps. Annex II of the regulation, which delimits the peninsular zones, names no place in Teruel province. Proposals on non-EU buyers made in 2025-26 were not tracked. Real Decreto 689/1978 art. 32 and Annex II (BOE consolidated); Ley 8/1975 art. 4; Código Civil art. 27
How a group can hold
Sociedad cooperativa (Aragón)
  • Sociedad cooperativa (Aragón). The Aragonese co-operatives law regulates and promotes co-operatives formed and operating in Aragón: societies that associate people for economic and social activities of common interest. It is the sourced collective vehicle here; other forms such as a SAT or an association were not opened. Decreto Legislativo 2/2014, texto refundido de la Ley de Cooperativas de Aragón, arts. 1 and 2 (BOE)
First claim
  • Derecho de abolorio: relatives to the fourth degree by the line the property came down, with the descendants of the seller who hold property of the same origin, may take rustic land and buildings that have stayed in the family for two generations before the seller's, by tanteo (30 natural days from a notified intention to sell) and failing that by retracto after a sale. It outranks every other pre-emption right except that of co-owners and public bodies (Decreto Legislativo 1/2011 arts. 588-590, 594 and 598).
  • Vacant immovables in Aragón belong by law to the Autonomous Community (Decreto Legislativo 1/2011 art. 598 bis, added by Ley 6/2021).
Direction

What the law rules out
  • A new isolated house is not allowed where a population nucleus can form (two or more residential buildings within a 150 metre radius, by default), and where one is allowed the default limit is one building per parcel of at most 300 square metres built on at least 10,000 square metres tied to it, so a new multi-household settlement on generic non-urban land is not a route (Decreto Legislativo 1/2014 art. 34.2).
  • Pueblos Vivos Aragón does not give financial aid and has no staff or information for immigration paperwork or arrival from abroad (its own page).
  • Historic communities and mancomunidades are run under their own statutes and customary rules (Ley 7/1999 art. 95), so a pasture or monte in their area is not simply a parcel on an open market.

Where legal and cost questions come first

These are the questions a community has to be able to answer in a place: who may hold the land, how a group can hold it together, and what it costs. Read them as context for a conversation with the people already there, not as a verdict on the place.

Legal and ownership

Foreign ownership
allowed The defence-zone limits on foreign buyers (Ley 8/1975, RD 689/1978 art. 32 and Annex II) name no place in Teruel province, and no other nationality-based bar on buying rustic or urban land was found in the texts read. Those limits are framed for buyers from outside the EU and cover islands, Cartagena, the Strait, the bay of Cadiz, the Portuguese and French frontier zones, Galicia and North Africa. 2025-26 national proposals on non-EU buyers were NOT verified. Newcomer-support programmes are narrower than the law: Pueblos Vivos Aragon serves EU nationals and people with Spanish work and residence permits.
Collective ownership path
Sociedad cooperativa (DL 2/2014, Ley de Cooperativas de Aragon), SAT or asociacion; rehabilitating a building in an aldea or deserted village is the planning route that allows division into several dwellings (DL 1/2014 art. 35.1.c)
Several households on one holding
with conditions
Residency needed to buy
no residency required
Where building is decided
Municipal licence under DL 1/2014 (Ley de Urbanismo de Aragon) and the municipal plan: on generic non-urban land one isolated house is allowed only where no population nucleus can form (by default, two or more residential buildings within a 150 m radius of the building's centre), with one building per parcel of at most 300 m2 built and a parcel of at least 10,000 m2 tied to it (art. 34.2); rehabilitation in aldeas, barrios or deserted villages by special authorisation may change use and divide into several dwellings (art. 35.1.c); the plan may be stricter
First-claim holders
Relatives to the fourth degree by line of origin, on rustic land and buildings held in the family for two generations (derecho de abolorio: 30-day tanteo, 90-day retracto, expires two years after sale); Co-owners (comuneros) and public bodies, whose pre-emption the abolorio does not override (DL 1/2011 art. 598)
Key restriction
A new multi-household settlement on non-urban land is effectively closed by the 150 m nucleus test and the one-house-per-parcel rule; the open doors are a municipality's own housing, a rented house, or rehabilitation of an old village building. Houses of unknown ownership and a rural housing shortage are reported locally (Eco de Teruel, 30 Sep 2026); ownerless (vacant) buildings in Aragon pass by law to the regional government (DL 1/2011 art. 598 bis, added by Ley 6/2021).
Legal direction
stable DL 1/2014 read as BOE consolidated text updated 2025-02-12; no pending amendment was found in the sources opened. Spain's 2025-26 debates on non-EU buyers (tax proposal, end of the golden visa) were NOT verified here.

Field reality, water and soil

Water source control

Rights regime
Spanish state public hydraulic domain (Ley de Aguas, RDL 1/2001): concessions and aquifer controls by the confederations of the Ebro, the Jucar and the Tajo; users of one intake must form a comunidad de usuarios (art. 81)
Rights holder
mixed
Single-entity control
moderate Control sits with three basin authorities, not a private owner; the risk is scarcity, not concentration: Aqueduct puts 99% of the footprint in the extremely high class for 2050. Under the Ley de Aguas the basin plan sets the order of use and a concession can be expropriated for a preferred use (art. 60), groundwater bodies at risk of not reaching good status can be declared, with a recovery programme and a users' community (art. 56), and the Government can impose measures in extraordinary drought (art. 58). The 2022-2027 plans for the Jucar, Tajo and Ebro were approved by RD 35/2023.
When water is short

Detail
Mean temperature in the footprint is about 10.3 C (1970-2000) against warmer lowlands within 120-150 km (Zaragoza, Valencia); the 640-1,700 m relief gives cold winters and cooler summers than the Ebro valley and the coast. Altitude is the only buffer named here: the interior has no sea or lake influence, and it swings between cold winters and hot dry summers.

Who made this

The framework was originated by Askja and is developed by a small working group of practitioners and researchers. The demonstration site was built by one member of that group, who holds the practitioner seat.

Members are named by first name and role only, as in the working documents; no surnames, handles or contact details appear anywhere on the site.

There is no public contact route yet; the working group will set one.

How to read it

  • Thresholds filter; they never score or rank.
  • Land standing is qualitative and never a filter.
  • Every value carries its source and vintage.
  • The rivers and contours drawn on this page are ornament, not surveyed. For geography, use the map and ask the people who live there.
