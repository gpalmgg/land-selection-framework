# Climate: Virginia Piedmont (Louisa, Fluvanna and Orange), USA

**Headline finding:** The footprint is warm-humid Piedmont, about 16 C mean annual temperature by 2041-2060 under SSP2-4.5 (17-model mean), roughly 2 C above what the Global Solar Atlas records for the same ground today (about 14.3 C). The models disagree by almost 3 C, so the number is a band, not a point.

**Key data point (with vintage):** WorldClim CMIP6 v2.1 BIO1, 2041-2060, SSP2-4.5, 17-GCM ensemble mean over the footprint (2.5 arc-minute grid, 229 cells touched): **16.06 C** (single-model range 14.68 to 17.55 C; 3x3-cell mean at 38.0 N, -78.2: 15.96 C). GFDL-ESM4 returned HTTP 404 from the WorldClim server and is excluded (17 of 18 models). Retrieved 2026-10-04. Present-day comparison: Global Solar Atlas TEMP mean over 59 footprint points 14.28 C (13.69 to 14.81).

**Supporting facts:**
- NOAA State Climate Summaries 2022, Virginia: temperatures have risen more than 1.5 F since the beginning of the 20th century; under a higher-emissions pathway "historically unprecedented warming is projected during this century"; droughts projected to be more intense; the number and intensity of extreme heat and extreme precipitation events are projected to increase; cold waves less intense. Virginia was affected by 82 of the 290 US billion-dollar disasters 1980-2020 (state-wide).
- Virginia hazards named by NOAA: severe thunderstorms, tornadoes, winter storms, tropical storms, hurricanes, droughts and heat waves.
- Elevation across the footprint is low: Global Solar Atlas elevation at the 59 sample points 68 to 171 m (mean 119 m). There is no altitude buffer.
- The 2011 Virginia earthquake (M5.8, 23 August 2011) was centred in Louisa County; it is a hazard to design for, not a climate variable.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** Read the 16.1 C as "a Piedmont that behaves like today's central North Carolina". Plan for heat and for intense rain rather than for cold. The footprint has no mountain or coastal moderation, so the buffering comes from forest canopy and from the Blue Ridge to the west, not from elevation.
