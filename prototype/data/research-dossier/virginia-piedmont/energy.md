# Energy: Virginia Piedmont (Louisa, Fluvanna and Orange), USA

**Headline finding:** A good, very even solar resource (1,493 kWh/kWp/yr footprint mean, a spread of only 24 kWh/kWp) and the one place on the slate where a community has spent fifteen years showing what living without fossil fuel looks like (Living Energy Farm, Louisa).

**Key data point (with vintage):** Global Solar Atlas long-term average PVOUT (PVOUT_csi, annual) sampled by the GSA API at 59 points on a regular grid inside the footprint, retrieved 2026-10-04: **mean 1,492.7, median 1,493.2, min 1,481.1, max 1,505.3 kWh/kWp** (p10 1,486.1, p90 1,499.4); at 38.0 N, -78.2: 1,495.2 kWh/kWp, GHI 1,560.6 kWh/m2, elevation 127 m, air temperature 14.1 C. Mean GHI over the 59 points 1,559.8 kWh/m2. An earlier reading by the research judges (1,480 at 38.0 N, -78.5) is consistent.

**Supporting facts:**
- Living Energy Farm (Louisa) is "completely off the grid" and aims to use no fossil fuels; its DC microgrid is 1.9 kW of photovoltaic capacity with nickel-iron batteries, and it states an electricity cost of about $25 per year against a $1,320 US average (its own page; a self-reported figure).
- North Anna Nuclear Generating Station (Louisa County, two Westinghouse PWRs, on-line 1978 and 1980, about 1.79 GW; licences renewed to 2058 and 2060; Dominion announced in July 2024 it was evaluating a small modular reactor site) sits on Lake Anna (Wikipedia, opened).
- The 59-point grid sits on low, flat to rolling ground (68 to 171 m), so shading depends on forest, not terrain.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** The resource is good and reliable, not exceptional; the lever here is demand, as Living Energy Farm shows. Grid interconnection and rural-county permitting rules for solar were not opened in this run.
