# Legal + Economic: Virginia Piedmont (Louisa, Fluvanna and Orange), USA

**Headline finding:** Virginia fee simple with a narrow foreign-adversary bar on agricultural land, a land-use value assessment that is a tax deferral with five-year roll-back, partition law that now requires appraisal and offers a co-owner buy-out, and community land held in practice by nonprofit or cooperative corporations. The deeper legal fact is whose land it is: Monacan and Mannahoac country, with Rassawek in Fluvanna, and no cession treaty for this footprint established in the sources opened.

**Key data point (with vintage):** Farmland price, USDA NASS Land Values 2025 Summary (August 2025), Virginia, dollars per acre: farm real estate **6,100** (+4.3 percent on 2024; 4,600 in 2021), cropland **6,170** (+4.0), pasture **5,300** (+4.1). In US dollars per hectare (x 2.47105): pasture 13,097; farm real estate 15,073; cropland 15,246. These are state-wide averages: **no county or Lake Anna waterfront figure was obtained**, so confidence for the three counties is LOW. The 2026 summary (published August 2026) was not reachable (404 at the 2025 URL pattern).

**Supporting facts:**
- Foreign ownership: Code of Virginia 55.1-508 (2023 cc. 765, 796; in force 1 July 2023): no "foreign adversary" (a person determined by the US Commerce Secretary under 15 C.F.R. 7.4) may acquire any interest in agricultural land, any contrary acquisition is void and title vests in the Commonwealth. Definitions in 55.1-507; annual state reporting from federal AFIDA data in 55.1-509. No general bar on other foreign individuals appeared in Chapter 5.1.
- Land-use value assessment: Code of Virginia 58.1-3230 defines agricultural, horticultural, forest and open-space use; 58.1-3237 sets roll-back taxes (five most recent complete tax years plus simple interest where there is no sliding-scale ordinance) when use changes. Louisa County: 5 acres for agricultural or horticultural use and 20 acres for forest, applications by 1 November, a $10 fee, "a tax deferral, not a discount" (Louisa Commissioner of the Revenue page). Fluvanna: land-use ordinance effective 1 January 1978 (county page). Orange: assessor FAQ lists "How are land use values determined?" (programme exists; page detail not read).
- Conservation easements: Virginia Conservation Easement Act (10.1-1009 and following); holders are 501(c)(3) charitable bodies or public bodies.
- Heirs' and co-owned property: any tenant in common or owner of an undivided estate may compel partition (8.01-81); the court must order an appraisal and determine fair market value first (8.01-81.1, added 2020 cc. 115, 193, amended 2023 c. 333); allotment to a co-owner who will pay the appraised price comes before sale (8.01-83); an ordered sale is open-market unless sealed bids or auction are better for the group (8.01-83.1). The article is not titled the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act in the Code and whether Virginia has formally adopted that Act was NOT verified. Documented loss of land among Black and Freedmen families in these counties was NOT sourced and is not asserted.
- Building: Louisa requires a land-disturbance permit at 10,000 sq ft or more and a state stormwater permit at one acre or more (Louisa Community Development). Zoning rules for multi-household or communal residence in Louisa, Fluvanna and Orange were NOT opened.
- Community land in practice: Acorn is organised as a Virginia corporation that owns all property (land, buildings, business), with 501(d) tax status (its Legal Status page).
- Monacan Indian Nation: federally recognised by Public Law 115-121 (29 January 2018); the Act's findings record the 1677 Treaty of Middle Plantation, a 1722 Treaty of Albany negotiated with the Iroquois, and state recognition in 1989; its service area is all land within 25 miles of the centre of Amherst and trust-land provisions cover Amherst County and two Rockbridge parcels. The nearest edge of the footprint is roughly 34 miles from the centre of Amherst (town-centre point approximate), outside that service area.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** The legal gate is not the statute, it is the neighbour. Never buy rural parcels around existing communities, and never bid for land held as undivided family property: any co-owner can force a partition sale. The right way in is a three-week visit, a Letter of Introduction and a place inside a community that already holds land.

**Land standing (qualitative, never scored):** see ../data.json extras.landStanding. Territory rests on the Monacan Indian Nation's own pages (monacannation.gov, to which monacannation.com redirects). "About Us": "in 1607, the James River Monacan (along with their Mannahoac allies on the Rappahannock River) controlled the area between the Fall Line in Richmond and the Blue Ridge Mountains"; "Our History": "a confederation ranging from the Roanoke River Valley to the Potomac River, and from the Fall Line at Richmond and Fredericksburg west through the Blue Ridge Mountains"; home page: "our ancestral territory includes Virginia west of the fall line of the rivers". Rassawek: "the ancestral capital of the Monacan Indian Nation, sits at the confluence of the James and Rivanna Rivers" (Cultural Heritage Partners); Wikipedia places Point of Fork, where those rivers meet, in Fluvanna County. Twin Oaks writes that it is "located on the traditional territory of the Monacan and Mannahoac peoples". No statement of "unceded" or "ceded by treaty" is made for this footprint.
