# Regenerative ecosystem: Virginia Piedmont (Louisa, Fluvanna and Orange), USA

**Headline finding:** Louisa County holds a cluster of long-running income-sharing and off-grid communities (Twin Oaks since 1967, Acorn since 1993, Living Energy Farm since about 2010), each with a published, structured visitor path, within a 100 km radius that also reaches Shannon Farm, Innisfree Village and Open Circle. The entry is to visit and be known, never to buy a parcel nearby.

**Key data point (with vintage):** Ten named intentional-community sites located within 100 km of 38.0 N, -78.2 in the FIC directory (ic.org; the GEN directory shares the same records), the Federation of Egalitarian Communities pages and OpenStreetMap, checked 2026-10-04 (**10 sites**; the list below is the evidence, there is no georeferenced query behind the count):
1. Twin Oaks Community, Louisa County (OSM node, 19.8 km; FIC and GEN listing; FEC member; founded 1967; about 85 adults and 15 children).
2. Living Energy Farm, Louisa (OSM node, 19.6 km; FIC; own page: Louisa, Virginia; nine long-term residents and children).
3. Community of Peace, Louisa (FIC listing, status Forming, founded 2002; "Louisa, Virginia", about 22 km from the county seat by town centre).
4. Acorn Community Farm, Louisa County (OSM locality node, 25.9 km; FIC; FEC member; founded 1993; 20 to 30 members per FIC).
5. Magnolia Collective, Louisa town (FIC, Forming household on 4 acres at the edge of Louisa, about 22 km).
6. Emerson Commons Cohousing, near Charlottesville (FIC, Established, 2014; within 100 km because Charlottesville is 24.5 km away and "near" is the listing's own word; exact address not given).
7. Innisfree Village, Crozet (FIC; 550-acre life-sharing farm since 1971; Crozet about 44 km).
8. Open Circle, Etlan (Madison County; FIC and FEC "community in dialogue"; six members; Etlan about 59 km).
9. Shannon Farm, Rockfish Valley (Nelson County; FIC; founded 1972; about 70 adults; Afton about 56 km).
10. Richmond Cohousing, Manchester, Richmond (FIC; 19 units, urban cohousing; about 85 km).
Not counted: Land of Promise Ecovilla ("40 minutes SE of Charlottesville", privately owned homestead, forming; location too loose), Sarah's Acres and Cambia (no location in the FIC text), Compersia (Washington DC, outside 100 km), and the Southern Exposure Seed Exchange (a business of Acorn, not a separate site).

**Supporting facts:**
- Twin Oaks: income-sharing, each member works 38.5 hours a week for housing, food, healthcare and personal spending money; three-week visitor programme is a prerequisite to applying for membership (sliding-scale fee of $50 to $250); no drop-in visits; a January 2026 electrical fire destroyed a residence for ten members, clothing, laundry and a wood shop, and the community states it is exploring new businesses after the loss of its hammock business (its own pages). Do not describe its businesses from older pages.
- Acorn: egalitarian, income-sharing, secular, consensus community in central Virginia; land and business held by one Virginia corporation; asks visitors to send a questionnaire first; its seed business (Southern Exposure Seed Exchange) came in 1999 and hosts the Heritage Harvest Festival in September (Acorn pages).
- Living Energy Farm: monthly tours (suggested donation $25, none turned away), volunteers 2 weeks to 3 months at 30 to 35 hours a week, membership after that; looking for partners to build affordable, energy-independent cooperative housing (its pages; call-for-partners PDF linked from its homepage, not opened).
- Federation of Egalitarian Communities lists Twin Oaks ("90 adults and 15 children") and the dialogue community Open Circle ("7 adults in rural Virginia"); the FEC page returned injected spam text on one fetch, which was discarded.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** Count is a floor of what this run could place by town, not a census (FIC withholds exact coordinates, so only communities with a stated place could be counted). Four of the ten are forming, a household, or urban cohousing; six are established rural communities. Visit-first norms are the rule in every one of them.
