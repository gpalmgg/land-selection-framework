# Legal + Economic — Finger Lakes (Cayuga and Seneca lake country), United States

**Headline finding:** New York places no nationality bar on holding real property: Real Property Law section 10 gives noncitizens the same capacity to take, hold and dispose of real property as citizens (text as shown on nysenate.gov, 2026-10-04). The regime that matters is local zoning plus the agricultural-districts system, and above all the standing of the Cayuga Nation, whose land claim failed in court (2005) but whose repurchases of land around Cayuga Lake continue in the same market. Farmland is moderately priced by the slate's bands (county averages USD 3,972-6,402 per acre, 2022).

**Key data point (with vintage):** USDA NASS Census of Agriculture 2022 (published February 2024), average value of agricultural land including buildings, USD per acre, year-end 2022: Schuyler 3,972; Tompkins 3,994; Cayuga 4,355; Seneca 4,610; Yates 6,402; New York State 4,102 (Quick Stats bulk file, rows "AG LAND, INCL BUILDINGS - ASSET VALUE, MEASURED IN $ / ACRE", total domain). Converted at 2.471 acres per hectare: USD 9,815 (Schuyler) to USD 15,820 (Yates) per hectare. USDA NASS Land Values 2026 Summary (July 2026), New York state average: farm real estate USD 4,500/acre (2022 3,720; 2025 4,300; +4.7 percent 2025-26), cropland 4,150 (+3.5), pasture 2,040 (+4.6). These are county or state averages of farms with buildings, not asking prices; lakefront, wine-trail and Ithaca-fringe parcels are not separated out and could not be priced from a source opened. Confidence: medium.

**Supporting facts:**
- Agricultural Districts Law, Agriculture and Markets Law Article 25-AA (section 300: "many of the agricultural lands in New York state are in jeopardy of being lost"). Section 301: "land used in agricultural production" means not less than seven acres used as a single operation, producing for sale crops, livestock or livestock products of average gross sales value of USD 10,000 or more (or under seven acres with USD 50,000 or more). Section 305-a: local governments "shall not unreasonably restrict or regulate farm operations within agricultural districts in contravention of the purposes of this article unless it can be shown that the public health or safety is threatened." Not opened: district-creation process, 8-year review, agricultural assessment.
- Zoning routes to collective living: Town Law section 278 (cluster development, "to enable and encourage flexibility of design ... to preserve the natural and scenic qualities of open lands"; the unit count may not exceed what standard zoning permits; the town board must authorise the planning board by local law and name the districts); Town Law section 261-b (incentive zoning: bonuses granted "on condition that specific physical, social, or cultural benefits or amenities would inure to the community"). Whether each of the five counties' towns has adopted either was not checked; this is a per-town question. EcoVillage at Ithaca is the working example of cohousing neighbourhoods (FROG, SONG, TREE) on 170 acres; its legal form (condominium, co-operative or other) was not verified.
- Conservation easements: ECL section 49-0303 (Article 49, Title 3) defines an easement limiting development or use to preserve "scenic, open, historic, archaeological, architectural, or natural condition" and lists who may hold one (not-for-profit conservation organisations, public bodies, soil and water conservation districts). EVI reports 50 acres of its 170 in a conservation easement; the Finger Lakes Land Trust reports 35,000+ acres protected.
- Federal layer: the Nonintercourse Act (Act of July 22, 1790; 25 U.S.C. section 177) "bars sales of tribal land without the acquiescence of the Federal Government" (quoted in Cayuga Indian Nation v. Pataki, 413 F.3d 266, citing Sherrill). The Treaty with the Six Nations, 7 Stat. 44 (11 November 1794; Kappler, Indian Affairs: Laws and Treaties, vol. 2), Article II: "The United States acknowledge the lands reserved to the Oneida, Onondaga and Cayuga Nations, in their respective treaties with the state of New-York, and called their reservations, to be their property; and the United States will never claim the same ... but the said reservations shall remain theirs, until they choose to sell the same to the people of the United States, who have the right to purchase."
- Case law (opened): City of Sherrill v. Oneida Indian Nation of New York, decided 29 March 2005 (Cornell LII, case no. 03-855): "The principle that the passage of time can preclude relief has deep roots in our law"; the Oneidas "cannot regain [the reins of government] through open-market purchases from current titleholders"; the opinion does not analyse the Cayuga claim. Cayuga Indian Nation of New York v. Pataki, 413 F.3d 266 (2d Cir. 2005), decided 28 June 2005 per the Harvard Caselaw Access Project copy (CourtListener lists 1 August 2005): the District Court had held the 1795 and 1807 treaties invalid under the Nonintercourse Act and entered judgment for USD 247,911,999.42; the Second Circuit, applying Sherrill, held the possessory land claim barred by laches, reversed and entered judgment for the defendants; one judge concurred in the judgment on possession but dissented from the dismissal of the money-damages claim. The claim concerned 64,015 acres, the "Original Reservation" on the shores of the northern end of Cayuga Lake. Certiorari history not verified (the Nation's page mentions a 2007 ruling; not opened).
- The Cayuga Nation's own account (cayuganation-nsn.gov/history-culture, opened 2026-10-04; quote as the Nation's account, not a legal conclusion): New York "entered into an illegal land agreement" in 1795 leaving the Nation 3,200 acres; another "illegal agreement" in 1805 sold the remaining 3,200 acres; in 1980 the Nation "filed a land claim ... to recover 64,015 acres around Cayuga Lake", which a US District Court "dismissed ... as time-barred under the principle of laches". Land acquired since 2003, by year (acres): 2003 112; 2005 17; 2006 30; 2007 358; 2008 284; 2009 13; 2010 454; 2011 49; 2012 2; 2013 1; 2014 8; 2017 3; 2019 2; 2021 26; 2022 50; 2023 74; 2024 11 (sum 1,494 acres). The first acquisition named is a gas station and convenience store in Union Springs. Discrepancies to flag: the Nation says 1805 where the Second Circuit opinion says 1807; the Nation says the claim was dismissed in 2001 where the Second Circuit opinion records a District Court liability finding and a USD 247.9 million damages award that it reversed on laches in 2005 (the date of the damages award is not stated in the text read). Both are quoted separately and not reconciled here.
- Contamination registry: NYS DEC Environmental Remediation Sites database (data.ny.gov dataset c6ci-rzpg, rows updated 2026): 85 distinct sites in the five counties (Tompkins 28, Cayuga 21, Seneca 18, Yates 13, Schuyler 5), including 54 State Superfund (HW) sites of which 8 are Class 02 ("significant threat"): General Electric Co. (Auburn), Cayuga County Groundwater Contamination (Union Springs), Brillo Landfill (Victory), Seneca Army Depot (Romulus), NYSEG Geneva Border City MGP (Waterloo), NYSEG Ithaca Court St. MGP, Morse Industrial Corporation (Ithaca), Mercury Aircraft (Dresden); also 13 Brownfield Cleanup Program, 8 Environmental Restoration, 7 Voluntary Cleanup and 3 RCRA records, among them the Former Ithaca Gun Factory (BCP, Active) and Ithaca Gun Company Site (VCP). The registry is public.
- Hydraulic fracturing: DEC SEQR Findings Statement of 29 June 2015 "officially prohibits HVHF in New York".

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** The legal question here is not whether a foreigner may buy but whether arriving is right at all. A purchase near Cayuga Lake competes in a live market with a Nation that was landless for over two centuries by its own account and has bought back about 1,500 acres. The honest entry is through existing consent-based structures (EcoVillage at Ithaca membership; Groundswell programmes), never through the Ithaca housing market, and never as a bidder for parcels the Nation may want.
