# Soil — Valle Maira, Italy

**Headline finding:** SoilGrids 2.0 gives a high topsoil organic carbon of about 59 g/kg (0–30 cm, depth-weighted) across the footprint, typical of cool, high alpine forest and grassland soils, and well inside the 0–150 slider range.

**Key data point (with vintage):** SoilGrids 2.0 SOC mean, depth-weighted over 0–5 (5 cm), 5–15 (10 cm) and 15–30 cm (15 cm) layers, 250 m pixels, 9,055 pixels in the footprint: **mean 58.6 g/kg, median 61.6 g/kg, p10–p90 41.1–72.5 g/kg, maximum 95.3 g/kg; 0 percent of pixels above 150 g/kg.** Layer means (g/kg): 0–5 cm 106.8; 5–15 cm 57.7; 15–30 cm 43.1. Mountain 11-comune sub-aggregate (7,827 pixels): mean 61.8, median 63.2, p10–p90 47.4–73.1. Read from files.isric.org soc_*cm_mean.vrt on 2026-10-04 (SoilGrids 2.0; dataset vintage as other cells, "2020").

**Supporting facts:**
- The 0–5 cm layer is more than twice the 15–30 cm layer: an organic-rich surface over thin mineral soil, which is what steep forest and pasture soils look like.
- No peat or organic-soil exceedance of the slider (no pixel above 150 g/kg).
- Pettenati (2013) describes organic farming and multifunctional agriculture on terraced sunny slopes; no soil survey was opened. IPLA (Regione Piemonte) soil maps and ESDAC were not opened.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** carbon content is not fertility or depth. Slope, stoniness and terrace condition decide what can be grown; SoilGrids at 250 m cannot see a terrace.
