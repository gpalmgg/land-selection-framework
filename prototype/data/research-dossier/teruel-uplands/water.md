# Water — Teruel uplands and the Maestrazgo, Spain (Aragon)

**Headline finding:** Under WRI Aqueduct 4.0 the footprint is in the extremely high water-stress class for 2050 on 99% of its area (area-weighted ratio 1.36, above the 0-1 slider). These are headwaters, but the headwaters are already fully drawn.

**Key data point (with vintage):** Aqueduct 4.0 future_annual bau50_ws_x_r (2050 business as usual), area-weighted over 6 HydroBASINS L6 basins covering 11,087 km2: **1.357**; minimum basin 0.441, maximum 5.855; 10,961 km2 Extremely high (>80%), 126 km2 High (40-80%). The river-side column is by river geography of each Aqueduct basin, not an overlay of the confederation boundaries (the MITECO WMS did not answer). Roughly half the area drains to the Ebro, roughly half to the Jucar district, a few percent to the Tajo and Cabriel headwaters.

**Supporting facts:**
- Pueblos Vivos Aragon (Sierra de Albarracin page, opened by the standing agent): the Tajo, the Guadalaviar/Turia and the Cabriel (a Jucar tributary) rise in the Sierra.
- The Ley de Aguas and RD 35/2023 (2022-2027 plans for the Jucar, Tajo and Ebro) set the legal frame: see legal.md and the water_source_control record.
- A Cuencas Mineras mayor wrote in September 2026 of a summer of both fire and flash flood (op-ed, Eco de Teruel, 11 Sep 2026: opinion, one voice).

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** A ratio above 1 means withdrawals exceed the renewable supply Aqueduct counts. For a new household the question is the municipal supply, a well licence from the confederation and the village's irrigation community, not the river. The Aqueduct unit is a ratio; it is shown as a native score and never a grade.
