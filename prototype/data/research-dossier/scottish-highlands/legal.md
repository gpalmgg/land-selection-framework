# Legal + Economic — Highlands (north-west Scotland), UK

**Headline finding:** No nationality bar on owning land, but ownership is the wrong door: arrival is by becoming a resident of a community landowner, taking a croft through the Crofting Commission, or renting social housing. Statutory rights to buy belong to communities, not to newcomers, and crofts and common grazings are regulated tenures.

**Key data point (with vintage):** Scottish Land Commission, Rural Land Market Data Report 2025 (sales 2020-2024, Registers of Scotland data), region Highlands and Islands (Argyll, Bute, Caithness, Inverness, Nairn, Orkney and Zetland, Ross and Cromarty, Sutherland; wider than the footprint): farmland, mean price per hectare GBP 5,140 (2020, 11 sales), 6,713 (2021, 18), 7,540 (2022, 29), 8,051 (2023, 23), **9,497 (2024, 17 sales, 1,590 ha)**; 2020-2024 mean 7,578 over 98 sales and 10,528 ha. Estates: 47 sales over 57,437 ha for GBP 115,865,548 in 2020-2024 (**about GBP 2,017 per ha**, derived: 115,865,548 / 57,437); 2024: 10 sales, 20,154 ha, GBP 36,067,580 (about GBP 1,790 per ha, derived). The report says croft tenancies and farmland bought for housing are excluded where possible; housing plots are not a series in it. The Land Commission notes the Highlands and Islands has the lowest per-hectare values in every year and attributes it to land capability.

**Supporting facts:**
- Community right to buy, Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003 Part 2: a right of first refusal on registered land, not a compulsory sale. Part 3 crofting community right to buy and, in the 2016 Act, Part 3A (abandoned, neglected or detrimental land) and Part 5 (furthering sustainable development) are compulsory purchases (gov.scot overview). Community Empowerment (Scotland) Act 2015 Part 5: asset transfer requests to relevant public authorities.
- Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2025 (asp 15), Royal Assent 16 December 2025: Part 1 large landholdings, Part 2 leasing. Section 47: Part 3 and section 11 (model leases) in force the day after Royal Assent; the rest by regulations. Commencement of the Part 1 duties was NOT verified, so none is relied on.
- Crofting: a crofter must be ordinarily resident on, or within 32 km of, the croft and cultivate and maintain it; a croft tenancy cannot be assigned without the Commission's consent (Crofters (Scotland) Act 1993 s.8), and the Commission is notified of a change of ownership (crofting.scotland.gov.uk, not crofting.scot).
- Planning: National Planning Framework 4 (13 February 2023) Policy 17 supports new rural homes on LDP-allocated sites, reused brownfield land or buildings, where necessary for a viable rural business or croft with an essential worker, and in remote rural areas where the home supports an existing fragile community and identified local housing outcomes; small-scale crofts and woodland crofts and resettlement of previously inhabited areas only where the local plan identifies them.
- Tax: Land and Buildings Transaction Tax non-residential rates (Revenue Scotland): 0 percent to GBP 150,000, 1 percent to GBP 250,000, 5 percent above (effective 25 January 2019). Residential and additional-dwelling rates were not opened.
- Inheritance tax agricultural property relief was reformed from April 2026 (UK-wide); the current allowance was not opened here.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** The harm notes govern: a group arriving to buy an estate is the pattern the buyouts exist to reverse. Entry means becoming a resident, taking the housing the trust offers and not outbidding it. Crofts and common grazings are not for sale to an outsider's plan, and a township with a live crofting right to buy is a township to leave alone. The land price series is a land series: it says nothing about the price or availability of a house, which is the actual gate.
