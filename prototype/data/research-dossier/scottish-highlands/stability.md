# Stability — Highlands (north-west Scotland), UK

**Headline finding:** No fatal conflict events lie within 200 km, and none within 300 km, in the UCDP record. The stability that matters here is housing and tenure stability: the population is roughly flat, old, and short of homes, and the land is held by few owners and a growing number of communities.

**Key data point (with vintage):** UCDP Georeferenced Event Dataset v25.1 (385,918 events, 1989-2024), great-circle distance (haversine, 6,371.0088 km) from (-5.1, 57.8): **0 events within 200 km in 2019-2024**; also 0 within 250 km and 300 km. The nearest event in the whole file is 297 km away (Ballycastle town, Northern Ireland, 1991). GED v26.1 (adds 2025): still 0 within 200 km for 2019-2025. Computed 2026-10-04.

**Supporting facts:**
- Population: NRS mid-year estimates on 2022 wards, three wards: 25,992 (2011), 25,803 (2016), 26,319 (2021). Scotland's Census 2022: 26,859 (via citypopulation.de, which attributes National Records of Scotland). By ward, Census 2011 to 2022: North, West and Central Sutherland 6,130 to 5,791; Wester Ross, Strathpeffer and Lochalsh 12,230 to 12,196; Caol and Mallaig 8,790 to 8,872. The Census and mid-year series differ in base: the shape (flat, with the Sutherland ward declining) is the same.
- Age: median age 47 (mid-2011), 50 (2016), 51 (mid-2021) against Scotland 41, 41, 42. Share aged 65 and over 21.5, 24.7, 26.2 percent against Scotland 16.9, 18.5, 19.6. Under-16s 16.9 to 13.8 percent (Scotland 17.3 to 16.6).
- Ownership: Scottish Land Commission homepage: 408 landowners own 50 percent of privately owned rural land in Scotland (attributed to Who Owns Scotland); Community Land Scotland: around 563,000 acres community-owned, over 140 member organisations (Scotland-wide, not footprint figures).
- Housing is the binding constraint: Assynt's 2019 survey found 188 of 698 homes (26.9 percent, 2011 Census) were second or holiday homes, against a Scottish average of 1.5 percent, and 38 respondents knew of kin or friends who left for want of a home (parish of Assynt, about 1,028 people; not a footprint figure).

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** An ageing, flat population means the places hold, and the rooted community is the stability. A group of newcomers is not a stabilising force there; one housing application taken from a local household is a loss.
