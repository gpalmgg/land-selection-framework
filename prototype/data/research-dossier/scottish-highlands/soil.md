# Soil — Highlands (north-west Scotland), UK

**Headline finding:** The soil carbon figure for this footprint is off the scale of the site's slider: SoilGrids 2.0 gives a depth-weighted 0-30 cm mean of about 305 g/kg, twice the slider's 150 g/kg end, because the ground is blanket peat, peaty podzol and peaty gley. That is a statement about peat, not about fertility.

**Key data point (with vintage):** SoilGrids 2.0 (ISRIC) soil organic carbon, mean, layers 0-5, 5-15 and 15-30 cm (250 m cells, Homolosine grid), read from the VRT files, sampled over the footprint (174,880 cells), depth-weighted by 5:10:15 cm and converted from dg/kg to g/kg. Layer means: 336.4 (0-5), 333.0 (5-15), 275.2 (15-30). Depth-weighted 0-30 cm: mean **304.7 g/kg**, median 303.4, 10th-90th percentile 221.1 to 393.7, maximum 524.9. 99.9 percent of cells exceed 150 g/kg and 95.7 percent exceed 200. Computed 2026-10-04; SoilGrids 2.0 is a 2020-vintage product.

**Supporting facts:**
- SoilGrids is a global statistical model, uncertain over organic soils; it is the same product used for the other regions, so the number is comparable, not independently validated. The Scottish Government's Carbon and Peatland 2016 map (classes 1 to 5 by peat and priority peatland habitat) exists and was opened, but the class areas inside this footprint were not computed.
- Contaminated land: Highland Council reports under Part IIA of the Environmental Protection Act 1990 that it has identified and remediated two contaminated sites in the whole council area, 25-71 Joss Street, Invergordon and Zone A, Rear Garden, Boxwood, Tain, both outside the footprint, and keeps a public register and inspection strategy (page last modified 2 July 2026). That is the formal statutory record, not a survey: sheep-dip, mining or military legacy was not examined.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** A peat-dominated soil is a carbon store that the wrong build, drain or track opens up: the number is a reason for restraint, not for growing. Mineral pockets (river terraces, machair, the lower glens) carry the gardens and the crofts, and they are a small fraction of the footprint; the median says the typical cell is organic-rich, the 10th percentile (221) says even the thin tail is not low. Cropland suitability and peat depth were not opened.
