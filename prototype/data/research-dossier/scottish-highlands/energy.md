# Energy — Highlands (north-west Scotland), UK

**Headline finding:** Solar is a minor resource: the footprint's modelled long-term PV output is about 816 kWh per kWp per year, just above the slider's 800 floor, with the glens in mountain shade running far lower. The energy story here is hydro, wind and community grids, not panels.

**Key data point (with vintage):** Global Solar Atlas long-term average, PVOUT_csi (kWh/kWp/yr), API api.globalsolaratlas.info/data/lta, queried at 339 grid points inside the footprint (0.06 degree latitude by 0.085 degree longitude steps, point-in-polygon) on **2026-10-04**: mean **815.6**, median 834.2, 10th-90th percentile 739.8 to 860.7, minimum 593.2, maximum 887.6. At the coordinates (-5.1, 57.8): 838.5 (global horizontal irradiation 795 kWh/m2 per the API; modelled elevation 407 m). Elevation across the sample points: 4 to 883 m.

**Supporting facts:**
- The pre-selection note quoted 907 kWh/kWp at 57.4 N, -3.9 W (judge E, not re-run); that point is east of the footprint, and it is not used.
- Knoydart Renewables, a subsidiary of the Knoydart Foundation, operates a hydroelectric scheme that supplies the Inverie area (knoydart.org). Eigg runs a renewable grid (Eigg Electric) and its Heritage Trust describes An Laimhrig as powered and heated by renewable energy (isleofeigg.org, May 2024); Eigg is a Small Isle masked from the cell, so it is context only. Neither scheme's capacity was opened.
- Community hydro and wind capacity for the footprint were not opened and are UNVERIFIED.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** The cell is a mean over a steep landscape: south-facing, unshaded sites will exceed it, north-side glens will fall well below. PV is a supplement; heat, grid connection and fuel are the real questions, and a community landowner's own energy scheme (where one exists) is the thing to learn about first.
