# Regenerative network — Highlands (north-west Scotland), UK

**Headline finding:** The directory count is small, two sites, and says little: the regenerative work here runs through community landowners and crofting townships that no directory lists as ecovillages. The cell counts only what the Global Ecovillage Network (GEN) directory and OpenStreetMap list inside 100 km, and every site is named.

**Key data point (with vintage):** Sites listed by GEN or OpenStreetMap within 100 km of (-5.1, 57.8), retrieved 2026-10-04: **2**. (1) The Park, Ecovillage Findhorn, Moray: GEN directory entry (founded 1962) and an OpenStreetMap node tagged identity=global_ecovillage_network (57.6519 N, -3.5918 W), 91.0 km. (2) Rubha Phoil, a nature-based permaculture community on the Isle of Skye (Sleat): GEN directory entry (founding year 2016); OpenStreetMap geocoding via Nominatim puts it at 57.0627 N, -5.8961 W, 94.8 km. Method: Overpass query for community=intentional, community=ecovillage, place=ecovillage, amenity=ecovillage, intentional_community, network=Global Ecovillage Network, identity=global_ecovillage_network, land_use/farm/permaculture within 100 km (returned only Findhorn); GEN directory keyword searches (Scotland, Highland, Findhorn, Skye, Forres, others) with each Scottish hit read; a name-regex Overpass pass (ecovillage, permaculture, cohousing, Camphill, intentional, Findhorn, Rubha Ph, Tamera, Trees for Life, community land; 82 elements) was run as a second check: it returned only the Findhorn complex (Findhorn Foundation, Cluny Hill College, Findhorn Ecovillage: one community, counted once), Rubha Phoil (OSM node, relation and the building 'Permaculture on the Edge' at the same Sleat site: counted once) and place-name noise; a building called 'Tamera Beg' at 65 km has no evidence of being a community and is not counted. Completeness is UNVERIFIED: the GEN directory has no spatial filter reachable without its map interface.

**Supporting facts:**
- Not counted (not listed by GEN or OpenStreetMap as ecovillages or intentional communities): Knoydart Foundation (community buyout, 1999, about 7,000 ha, 91 km), Assynt Foundation (Glencanisp and Drumrunie, 2005, 44,400 acres, 39 km), Applecross Community Company, Coigach Community Development Company, Isle of Eigg Heritage Trust (119.5 km, outside the radius), Trees for Life (Dundreggan, 68 km; a restoration programme, not a settlement), and the 140-plus Community Land Scotland member bodies. The Coigach and Assynt Living Landscape (Scottish Wildlife Trust, 63,500 ha) is a partnership, not a site.
