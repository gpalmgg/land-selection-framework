# Soil — Downeast Maine (Washington County), USA

**Headline finding:** The county's topsoil is carbon-rich: a depth-weighted 55 g/kg over 0-30 cm, driven by a very organic surface layer (156 g/kg in the top 5 cm), with no part of the county above the 150 g/kg slider ceiling.

**Key data point (with vintage):** **55 g/kg**, SoilGrids 2.0 (ISRIC, 2020 product), soil organic carbon mean at 250 m, depth-weighted 0-30 cm (5/10/15 cm layers) over 102,933 land cells (6,433 km2): mean 54.7, median 55.4, p10 41.2, p90 66.4, max 97.7 g/kg; 0% above 150. Layer means 155.6 (0-5 cm), 46.7 (5-15 cm), 26.5 (15-30 cm).

**Supporting facts:**
- Land in farms is 121,714 acres, 7.4% of the county's land area (about 1.64 million acres); cropland 39,623 acres, woodland within farms 70,294 acres (USDA Census of Agriculture 2022).
- Blueberries occupy 35,143 acres, the largest crop by acreage; forage 1,281 acres. Wild blueberry is a native lowbush plant in a two-year prune-and-crop cycle, grown on glacial outwash plains and uplands; UMaine Extension records that it was first grown at scale by Wabanaki people who burn-pruned and harvested fields.
- Irrigated land in farms is 12,958 acres, 11% of land in farms (USDA 2022 county profile).
- Contamination: no county-specific finding was opened. Statewide, Maine's PFAS farm problem is tied to licensed biosolids spreading sites; MFT and MOFGA run PFAS testing grants for farms and waive commercial-farm criteria for Indigenous producers of food and medicine. Maine CDC says 1 in 10 Maine wells has too much arsenic.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** SoilGrids is a 250 m model, not a soil survey; a parcel needs the NRCS Web Soil Survey (SSURGO) and a lab test. The blueberry barrens and the PFAS and pesticide questions are for a site-level look, which this tool does not do.
