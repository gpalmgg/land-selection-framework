# Stability — Downeast Maine (Washington County), USA

**Headline finding:** No organised-violence events are recorded within 200 km of the county in 2019-2024. The stability questions here are not about conflict: they are tribal-state law in motion, a housing market under strain, and a coast that floods.

**Key data point (with vintage):** **0 events**, UCDP GED v25.1 (385,918 events, 1989-2024), haversine distance 200 km from [-67.6, 44.9], 2019-2024. Also 0 within 250 and 300 km and 0 in all years within 200 km. GED v26.1 (adds 2025): 0 within 300 km for 2019-2025. Nearest event of any year: 352.9 km (Quebec City area, 1995-1998). The 200 km circle reaches New Brunswick; no event there either.

**Supporting facts:**
- Tribal-state law: the 1980 Maine Indian Claims Settlement Act (Pub. L. 96-420, 25 U.S.C. 1721 et seq.) and the Maine Implementing Act (30 MRS ch. 601) frame jurisdiction. In 2020 a task force of legislators and Wabanaki Chiefs made 22 consensus recommendations. In the 132nd Legislature (2025-26), LD 395 created a working group to report by 2 December 2026, LD 785 was amended considerably in committee, LD 870 added a Wabanaki seat to the Land Use Planning Commission and LD 1054 made tribes eligible easement holders (Wabanaki Alliance scorecard). The 133rd Legislature begins in January 2027.
- Housing: the Sunrise County Economic Council estimates that 59% of Washington County households cannot afford to buy a home (June 2026), and describes shrinking vacancy, short-term rental pressure and hidden homelessness.
- Climate: flooding in Machias during the January 2024 storms; a sea level rise rate 75% faster over 1996-2025 than the long-term rate; groundwater drought through winter 2025-26 (statewide).
- Funding: Maine Farmland Trust cut ten positions in July 2026 and says some programs will scale back; federal funding is described as uncertain. Institutions named in this dossier can change.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** A zero here is a statement about armed conflict only; it should not be read as 'stable' in the wider sense. UCDP counts only organised violence with a fatality.
