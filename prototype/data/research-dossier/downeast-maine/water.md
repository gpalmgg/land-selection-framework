# Water — Downeast Maine (Washington County), USA

**Headline finding:** On the global surface-water metric the county is about as water-secure as anywhere in the slate, yet the people who live here depend on private wells, and Maine's groundwater was still in drought through winter 2025-26. Two things are true at once: the Aqueduct score is very low, and wells are the exposure.

**Key data point (with vintage):** **0.009** (raw 0-1 ratio), WRI Aqueduct 4.0, bau50_ws_x_r (2050 business as usual), area-weighted over the 2 HydroBASINS level-6 basins that intersect the county (basin 731110: 0.0084, 92.5% of intersected area; basin 731120: 0.0211). Both are category Low (<10%). Baseline 0.0087. Consistent with the map polygon at the coords (score 0.0, Low).

**Supporting facts:**
- Private wells are unregulated in Maine; owners are responsible for testing and treating. Maine CDC advises annual tests for coliform, E. coli, nitrate and nitrite, and every 3-5 years for arsenic, uranium, radon and others.
- More than half of Maine relied on private wells in 2023 (STS 2026, statewide). Many USGS monitoring wells reached record monthly lows in fall 2025; 3 of 18 fell below the 5th percentile and had not recovered; Maine remained in hydrologic drought through winter 2025-26 (statewide, not county).
- Great ponds are inland waters over 10 acres in a natural state (38 MRS 480-B); the State declares itself trustee of its waters for Shoreland Zoning, which reaches 250 feet from great ponds, rivers and saltwater and 75 feet from streams (38 MRS 435).
- The St. Croix is a border river; the St. Croix International Waterway Commission has eight commissioners appointed by the Governor of Maine and the Premier of New Brunswick.
- Tidal and coastal water: the Maine Indian Tribal-State Commission regulates fishing on certain waters within and bordering Indian territory under the 1980 Settlement Act; the Wabanaki Nations' fisheries history is the subject of its 2022 Sea Run report.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** Aqueduct measures surface withdrawals against supply, not well yield or groundwater drought; do not treat 'Low' as an assurance for a household well. Water doctrine for private withdrawals was not opened and is not claimed.
