# Climate — Downeast Maine (Washington County), USA

**Headline finding:** A cold-water coast with a warming trend that is real but not simple. Over Washington County, the 17-model WorldClim CMIP6 ensemble puts mean annual temperature for 2041-2060 (SSP2-4.5) at 9.0 C, with models spread from 7.8 C to 10.7 C. The Gulf of Maine beside it has warmed nearly three times faster than the world ocean since 1982, though it has cooled somewhat in the past couple of years, which Maine's climate scientists call a pause, not a reversal.

**Key data point (with vintage):** **9.0 C**, WorldClim CMIP6 v2.1, BIO1, 2041-2060, SSP2-4.5, 17-GCM mean of footprint zonal means (579 land cells at 2.5 arc-minutes; sd 0.77 across models; value at coords 9.06). Retrieved 2026-10-04. GFDL-ESM4 returned HTTP 404 for this tile, so 17 of 18 listed models are used.

**Supporting facts:**
- Maine has warmed about 3.5 F since the late 1800s; the 10 warmest years on record have all occurred since 1998; annual precipitation is up about 15% over the last century (Maine Climate Council STS Interim Climate Science Report, April 2026).
- Gulf of Maine sea surface temperature rose 0.84 F per decade over 1982-2024, nearly triple the world ocean's 0.3 F per decade; 2024 was the 12th warmest year on record at 51.51 F (GMRI 2024 Warming Update).
- The STS 2026 report says Gulf waters have generally stayed above the long-term average since 2010 and that the recent cooling is best read as a pause in warming rather than a reversal.
- Sea level rise along the Maine coast has nearly doubled in rate over the last 30 years (75% faster in 1996-2025 than the long-term rate at four gauges including Eastport). A new tide gauge was installed in Machias in September 2024; it recorded the 10 and 13 January 2024 storm surges that flooded Machias.
- Blacklegged ticks and tick-borne disease (Lyme, anaplasmosis, babesiosis) have risen in Maine over two decades; Maine had the third-highest Lyme rate in the US in 2023 (STS 2026, citing US CDC).
- The 2026 STS report opens on the January 2026 deep-cold event: air temperatures below -30 C came from the Arctic via a wavy jet stream, not from Maine. Warming and brutal cold spells coexist.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** The 9.0 C figure is a land-only zonal mean on an 18 km-class grid; ocean cells are nodata, and a coastal fringe and an inland barren differ in ways this raster cannot show. Do not read the figure as a ceiling on heat or a floor on cold. National and state services (Maine Climate Council, GMRI) are context only and are not relabelled as the WorldClim scenario.
