# Legal — Bas-Saint-Laurent (St. Lawrence south shore), Canada (Québec)

**Headline finding:** Farmland here is governed by the CPTAQ regime (Québec's agricultural-land protection law), tightened by Bill 86 (SQ 2025, c. 5). Non-residents of Québec need CPTAQ authorization to acquire farm land of four hectares or more; Bill 86 adds investment funds and holdings above a regulated cap, and (interim, until a decree) buyers who will not register a farm operation within 1 km of an urbanization perimeter in two of the eight MRCs, Rimouski-Neigette and Rivière-du-Loup. The other six MRCs are in the Act's interim group F, where that rule does not apply. The region has the strongest official welcome infrastructure in the project: an agent in every MRC, Place aux jeunes, and ARTERRE farm-succession matching. French is the working language.

**Key data point (with vintage):** Bill 86, "An Act mainly to improve the legislative framework regarding the preservation of agricultural land and agricultural activities and to amend other legislation", SQ 2025, c. 5, assented 25 March 2025, official text opened 2026-10-04 (https://www.publicationsduquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fileadmin/Fichiers_client/lois_et_reglements/LoisAnnuelles/en/2025/2025C5A.PDF):
- New s. 79.0.6 of the Act respecting the preservation of agricultural land and agricultural activities (CQLR c. P-41.1): "it is prohibited to directly or indirectly acquire farm land without the authorization of the commission if (1) the acquirer is an investment fund ...; (2) the acquirer is not an agricultural operation registered in accordance with section 36.0.1 of the [MAPAQ Act] and the farm land is situated in the territory of a metropolitan community or a regional county municipality included in any of the groups identified in the decree made under section 79.0.5 and is 1,000 metres or less from an urbanization perimeter; or (3) the acquisition results in bringing the total area of the farm lands ... to an area that is greater than the total or annual area determined by government regulation."
- s. 79.0.12: authorization "is granted when the applicant declares that the farm land will, in the year following registration of the acquisition in the land register, be part of an agricultural operation registered in his name" (applies to ground (2) only).
- s. 105.5 (transitional): until the first decree under s. 79.0.5, ground (2) applies to "the municipalities included in groups A to D listed in Schedule B". Schedule B group D (MRC whose urban centre exceeds 20,000 people) includes MRC de Rimouski-Neigette and MRC de Rivière-du-Loup. Group F (demographic decline, urban centre under 20,000) includes MRC de La Mitis, Kamouraska, Témiscouata, des Basques, La Matanie and La Matapédia. No decree under s. 79.0.5 was found in this run (UNVERIFIED whether one has been made since March 2025).
- Grounds (1) and (2) of s. 79.0.6 have effect from 5 December 2024 (s. 113); ground (3) and the cap come into force with the first regulation under it (s. 114(2)); s. 109 puts investment funds under the prohibition from 5 December 2024 until a regulation defines them.
- Act respecting the acquisition of farm land by non-residents (CQLR c. A-4.1), as amended by Bill 86 s. 1-2: "farm land" means land "equal to or greater than four hectares or than any other smaller area that the Government may determine by regulation"; s. 10 now covers a non-resident acquiring shares in a business corporation "one of whose assets is farm land" (was "whose principal asset is"). The explanatory notes: the Act "grant[s] the Government the power to lower, by regulation, the threshold of four hectares required to subject the acquisition of farm land by a non-resident to the authorization of the commission". The full definition of "non-resident" in s. 1 of A-4.1 was not opened (legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca returned 403 and 502 to this run); Bill 86's own notes speak of "a person who does not reside in Québec".

**Supporting facts:**
- Federal Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act: the repository's own Nova Scotia and Eastern Townships dossiers cite it as extended to 1 January 2027 and as applying only in census metropolitan areas and agglomerations. canada.ca and justice.gc.ca pages returned 404 to this run; carried over, UNVERIFIED here.
- Civil-law freehold with land-register publicity; the notarial-deed requirement and the Civil Code collective vehicles (co-operative, association, trust under CCQ arts. 1260-1298, emphyteusis) are carried over from the Eastern Townships record and not re-opened here (legisquebec 403/502, CanLII 403).
- Land cost: no regional price figure that is both official and current was found. The only regional figure found is the La Financière agricole du Québec quote in Radio-Canada, 16 December 2019 (https://ici.radio-canada.ca/nouvelle/1428092/terres-agricoles-prix-bas-saint-laurent-arterre-transfert-fermes, opened): 6,000-6,500 CAD per hectare in Bas-Saint-Laurent versus 20,000-22,000 provincially, "Montérégie (highest) over 30,000". It is 2019 and stale; it is NOT shipped as a price. Province-wide context: Statistics Canada table 32-10-0047-01 (value per acre of farm land and buildings, July 1): Québec 6,656 (2019), 8,072 (2021), 9,973 (2023), 10,641 (2024), 11,131 CAD per acre (2025), +67 percent over six years, +4.6 percent 2024 to 2025; FCC 2025 report page: Québec +4.8 percent for 2025, no sub-provincial breakdown (https://www.fcc-fac.ca/en/knowledge/economics/farmland-values-report, opened).
- Welcome infrastructure (https://www.bas-saint-laurent.org/fr/setablir/accueil-integration.html, opened 2026-10-04): Place aux jeunes for 18-35 (including immigrants with a valid work permit); dedicated resources for 36 and over and personalised immigrant support; an organisation named for each MRC and each stream (for example Kamouraska: ProjeKtion 16-35, Arrimage Kamouraska, SAPI Kamouraska; Rimouski-Neigette: La Sphère de l'Emploi, none listed for 36+, AIBSL; La Matanie: Espace Avenir, none listed for 36+, SANAM). Financial help listed: moving-expense deduction, first-home tax credit, relocation support, medical and nursing loan forgiveness for rural placements. The page does not mention families, groups or farm succession.
- Place aux jeunes en région (https://placeauxjeunes.qc.ca/, opened): "le service est offert aux 18-35 ans"; the network offers free exploratory stays "que ce soit en famille ou en solo". A group or family arrival is therefore a stated fit limit for the service, though a family can visit.
- ARTERRE (https://www.arterre.ca, opened): "un service de maillage axé sur l'accompagnement et le jumelage entre aspirants-agriculteurs et propriétaires", run by the CRAAQ, province-wide across 17 regions, a local agent in each participating MRC; Bas-Saint-Laurent is listed. No matching statistics on the page. Radio-Canada (16 December 2019): in its first year in the region 150 registrants (70 owners, 35 aspiring farmers fully enrolled), one transfer completed, which was a lease and not a sale, about 30 farm visits, 10+ files in negotiation; about half of buyer applicants came from outside the region.
- Cultiver le Bas-Saint-Laurent / Table de concertation bioalimentaire du Bas-Saint-Laurent (https://www.tcbbsl.org/, opened): runs "Transférer sa ferme" farm-transfer days (27 January and 3 February 2026). Support structure, not a site.

**Land standing (qualitative; never scored):**
- Territory, Wolastoqiyik: the Wolastoqiyik Wahsipekuk First Nation (formerly Malécites de Viger) is, per Québec's own nation profile, the only Wolastoqey nation in Québec, with the Kataskomiq reserve in Whitworth township near Rivière-du-Loup and a small reserve lot at Cacouna (https://www.quebec.ca/en/government/quebec-at-a-glance/first-nations-and-inuit/profile-of-the-nations/malecite, opened). The Nation's own site (https://wolastoqiyikwahsipekuk.ca/, opened; note the host has no hyphen, the hyphenated name in the brief did not resolve) says "Plusieurs membres de la Nation se rendent toujours sur le Wolastokuk, territoire ancestral" and records "la perte des terres de réserve de Viger en 1869". Témiscouata as traditional Maliseet (Wolastoqiyik) territory: Burke, "Temiscouata: Traditional Maliseet Territory and Connections between the St. Lawrence Valley and the St. John River Valley" (Papers of the Algonquian Conference, Université de Montréal author; OCR copy dated 2016; https://ojs.library.carleton.ca/index.php/ALGQP/article/download/420/324/, opened): "Temiscouata falls between these two areas and historically it too has been part of traditional Maliseet territory".
- Territory, Mi'gmaq: Gespe'gewa'gi ("the last land"), the seventh district of Mi'gma'gi, is held by the Mi'gmaq communities of Listuguj, Gesgapegiag and Gespeg, tribal council Mi'gmawei Mawiomi Secretariat. Listuguj's own site (https://listuguj.ca/about-listuguj/, opened): "Gespe'gewa'gi has been Mi'gmaq and Listugujewaq traditional territory since time immemorial. It includes what is now known as the Gaspé Peninsula, parts of mainland Québec and Maine, and northeastern New Brunswick." Mi'gmawei Mawiomi (https://www.migmawei.ca/2026/06/300-years-of-peace-and-friendship-migmagi-is-unceded/, published 4 June 2026, opened): "Mi'gma'gi remains unceded territory" and "our ancestors never ceded or sold a single inch of land, water, or resources"; its home page calls Gespe'gewa'gi "the ancestral and unceded territory of the Mi'gmaq". Neither page names the Matapédia valley, so that the La Matapédia MRC lies within what these nations describe is NOT confirmed from a nation source; Listuguj is at the mouth of the Restigouche, which the Matapédia flows into. UNVERIFIED for the MRC, verified for the nation and the unceded position.
- Unceded for the Wolastoqiyik: no nation page opened states it; no cession treaty was found. The project's North American convention treats the territory as unceded; the entry says so with that framing.
- The title cases in the New Brunswick press (Wolastoqey and Mi'gmaq title litigation) are New Brunswick litigation; their outcomes are not transferred to Québec.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** Entry is by relationship and role: a farm-succession or lease through ARTERRE, a service job through the MRC agent, a village home. Place aux jeunes is youth-focused, so a family or a group is outside what it serves. "Not there" for Crown-land claims, for arrival without French, and for treating Gaspésie as part of this region.
