# Soil — Bas-Saint-Laurent (St. Lawrence south shore), Canada (Québec)

**Headline finding:** Carbon-rich humid-forest soils: SoilGrids 2.0 gives a footprint mean of 58.8 g/kg organic carbon over 0-30 cm (median 58.3), in the upper part of the project's 0-150 g/kg range, with no cells above 150 g/kg.

**Key data point (with vintage):** 58.8 g/kg SOC, SoilGrids 2.0 (ISRIC, 250 m), depth-weighted 0-30 cm = (0-5 cm x 5 + 5-15 cm x 10 + 15-30 cm x 15) / 30, footprint mask (352,473 cells). Median 58.25; p10-p90 43.1-75.1; maximum 121.1 g/kg; share of cells above 150 g/kg: 0. Layer means (dg/kg): 0-5 cm 964, 5-15 cm 706, 15-30 cm 384. Retrieved 2026-10-04 from files.isric.org (soc_{0-5,5-15,15-30}cm_mean.vrt). SoilGrids 2.0 is the 2020 release.

**Supporting facts:**
- The 0-5 cm layer alone averages 96.4 g/kg; the cell uses the 0-30 cm convention used for every other region.
- SoilGrids models forest organic horizons and podzols coarsely and carries wide uncertainty in humid forest; the value is a model estimate, not a sampling mean. The brief named Soil Landscapes of Canada and IRDA as comparator families used for the Nova Scotia and Eastern Townships cells; neither was opened here (those cells' own re-verification found them unreproducible from SoilGrids: Eastern Townships 53 g/kg versus a stated 32).
- Soil-type narrative (podzols, clay lowland, foothill ridges) was not verified from an opened source in this run and is not stated as fact.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** A high SOC figure on forested ground is mostly litter and organic horizons that disturbance releases; it is not a promise of arable productivity. Farm succession and village-edge roles (see legal.md) are the realistic way to meet the cultivated soils.
