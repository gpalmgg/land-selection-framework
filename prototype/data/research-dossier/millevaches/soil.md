# Soil — Plateau de Millevaches (Limousin), France

**Headline finding:** Acid granite soils under moor, grassland, conifer plantation and peat carry a high topsoil carbon stock. The mean sits well inside the slider range; peat bogs lift individual pixels but nothing approaches 150 g/kg.

**Key data point (with vintage):** SoilGrids 2.0 soil organic carbon, depth-weighted 0–30 cm (0–5, 5–15, 15–30 cm layers weighted 5:10:15), 250 m pixels, 53,241 pixels inside the footprint: mean **47.0 g/kg**, median 47.0, p10 38.9, p90 55.1, maximum 74.5; 0 percent of pixels above 150 g/kg. The 0–5 cm layer alone averages 99.7 g/kg. Retrieved 2026-10-04.

**Supporting facts:**
- Peatland is 3.4 percent of the Parc and wetlands about 20 percent (Parc); the SoilGrids mean therefore blends mineral soils and organic soils at 250 m resolution and cannot resolve a single bog.
- Granite parent material and the Parc's heath and moor suggest acid soils; pH and texture were not extracted here.
- Former uranium workings and radon on granite are common concerns in the Limousin; neither was verified inside this perimeter, so no contamination claim is made.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:**
A high carbon number on a plateau with peat is a reason for care, not a reason to dig: bog is stored carbon that the wrong foundation or drain opens up. A field test and the commune's wetland inventory matter more than the regional mean.
