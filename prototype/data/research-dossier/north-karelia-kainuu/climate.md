# Climate — North Karelia and Kainuu, Finland

**Headline finding:** Cold, continental boreal climate that warms by roughly two degrees by mid-century; the footprint mean annual temperature in 2041-2060 is about 5.4 °C under SSP2-4.5, and the largest change falls in the dark half of the year.

**Key data point (with vintage):** WorldClim CMIP6 v2.1 BIO1, 2041-2060, SSP2-4.5, 17-model ensemble mean of footprint zonal means = **5.37 °C** (spread across models 4.03 to 7.42, sd 0.90; 22-model mean 5.36). North Karelia part 5.65, Kainuu part 4.89, value at the region coordinates 5.67. Retrieved and computed 2026-10-04 on the 10 arcmin (about 18 km) grid supersampled to 0.01 degrees inside the 18 municipal polygons.

**Supporting facts:**
- FMI: North Karelia annual mean 1991-2020 about +2 to +4 °C, coldest in the north-east; coldest month about -9.5 °C in the south-west to -11 °C at the eastern border; warmest month July about +16 to +17 °C; 12-13 hot days a summer near lakes. Kainuu is described as typically continental.
- FMI scenarios (CMIP5, RCP2.6 to 8.5, context only): warming this century of about 1.8-5.6 °C (North Karelia) and 1.9-5.8 °C (Kainuu) against 1981-2010, largest from November to March; 1991-2020 already about 0.6 °C warmer than 1981-2010; annual precipitation +6 to +16% (North Karelia), +6 to +17% (Kainuu).
- Snow: permanent snow cover arrives in the last two weeks of November; mid-March depth 50-70 cm (70-80 cm on the uplands); FMI seasons page gives 60-90 cm for eastern and northern Finland.
- North Karelia lies mostly in the middle-boreal zone; the Maanselkä and Karjalanselkä uplands are northern boreal (FMI).

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** The 18 km grid blurs the lake-valley and upland contrast that FMI describes, so the footprint mean hides a real gradient between Pielinen and Höytiäinen lake country and the colder watersheds. Do not read the CMIP5/RCP scenario numbers as SSP2-4.5: they are a different model generation and a different baseline.
