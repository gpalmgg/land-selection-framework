# Accessibility and Demographics — Northeast Missouri / Southeast Iowa, United States

**Headline finding:** Very low density (9.2 persons per km2 across seven counties; 6.2 without Lee County), a population falling since 2010, and a hospital within 15 km of the region point.

**Key data point (with vintage):** Census 2020 counts (TIGERweb, PL 94-171): Lee 33,555; Appanoose 12,317; Davis 9,110; Van Buren 7,203; Clark 6,634; Scotland 4,716; Schuyler 4,032; total 77,567 over 8,418.1 km2 of land = 9.21 persons per km2. Census 2010 total 81,485, so -4.8 percent; ACS 2020-2024 5-year sum 76,874. GHSL R2023A 2030 projection over the footprint: 83,501 persons, 9.7 per km2 (GHSL's 2020 layer sums to 81,506, about 5 percent above the Census count, so its 2030 figure leans high).

**Supporting facts:**
- 2010 to 2020 change: Schuyler -9.0, Clark -7.1, Lee -6.4, Van Buren -4.8, Appanoose -4.4, Scotland -2.6, Davis +4.1 percent.
- Median age (ACS 2020-2024): Van Buren 44.7, Lee 43.6, Appanoose 43.5, Clark 43.5, Schuyler 39.2, Scotland 36.5, Davis 36.3; United States 39.2.
- Hospitals (OpenStreetMap snapshot, geodesic km from the region point): Scotland County Hospital, Memphis MO 14.5; Van Buren County Hospital, Keosauqua 26.5; Davis County Hospital, Bloomfield 45.4; Keokuk Area Hospital 52.8; Fort Madison Community Hospital 53.9; Mercy Medical Center, Centerville 77.7. 3 within 50 km, 20 OSM points within 100 km (about 18 institutions).
- Dancing Rabbit describes Memphis, Missouri, 13 miles north, as the county seat and "economic catch basin" with half of Scotland County's population; its school-age children attend the Memphis public school (about 12 miles) or learn at home.
- Fairfield (Jefferson County, Iowa; Maharishi International University) is inside the 100 km radius but outside the footprint.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** Geodesic distance is not drive time. The Census figures describe shrinking farm towns; the notes on how newcomers are received by neighbours are unverified here, and Dancing Rabbit's own account ("people here are generally friendly and open") is one side of that relationship.
