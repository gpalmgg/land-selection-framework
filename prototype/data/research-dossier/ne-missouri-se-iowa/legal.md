# Legal + Economic — Northeast Missouri / Southeast Iowa, United States

**Headline finding:** Two states, two foreign-ownership regimes, one border: Iowa bars nonresident aliens, foreign businesses and foreign governments from acquiring agricultural land (Iowa Code ch. 9I); Missouri allows foreign ownership only up to one percent of the state's agricultural acreage and routes sales to the Department of Agriculture for review (RSMo 442.571), with a 2024 executive order adding a foreign-adversary bar near military facilities. Community land trusts incorporated as nonprofits are exempt from both states' corporate-farming bars.

**Key data point (with vintage):** Farmland prices, USD per acre: Iowa farm real estate 10,100 and Missouri 5,200 (USDA NASS Land Values 2026 Summary, 31 Jul 2026; +3.2 and +4.0 percent); Missouri pasture 2,850; Iowa counties on 1 Nov 2025 (ISU Land Value Survey): Appanoose 6,679, Davis 7,735, Van Buren 8,438, Lee 10,222, each slightly below 2024. In USD per hectare (/0.404686): about 7,040 (Missouri pasture) to 25,260 (Lee). Confidence: medium; no county-level figures were found for Schuyler, Scotland or Clark (Missouri).

**Supporting facts:**
- Iowa Code 2026 ch. 9I (opened, PDF dated 9 Dec 2025): 9I.3(1) a nonresident alien, foreign business or foreign government, or its agent, trustee or fiduciary, shall not purchase or otherwise acquire agricultural land; holdings from 1 Jan 1980 may continue. 9I.1(7) a nonresident alien is a person who is neither a US citizen nor a lawful permanent resident. Exceptions (9I.3(3)): devise or descent (divest within two years, 9I.5), security interests, debt collection (sell within two years), research, up to 320 acres for an immediate or pending non-farming use (9I.4: convert within five years), and a foreign business meeting section 15.498. 9I.7 registration with the secretary of state; 9I.10 enforcement and 9I.11 escheat; 9I.12 civil penalty up to 25 percent of assessed value. Amended by 2024 Acts ch. 1007 and ch. 1090.
- RSMo 442.571 (opened 2026-10-04, effective 28 Aug 2015, histories L. 1978 S.B. 685, A.L. 2013 S.B. 9, A.L. 2015 S.B. 12): no alien or foreign business shall acquire agricultural land if aggregate alien and foreign ownership exceeds one percent of the state's agricultural acreage; a sale goes to the director of agriculture for review only if no completed IRS Form W-9 is signed by the purchaser; rulemaking authority to the department. No 2025 or 2026 amendment appears in the revisor's chapter 442 index.
- Missouri Executive Order 24-01 (Governor Parson, 2 Jan 2024, opened): orders emergency rulemaking so proposed foreign acquisitions are submitted before closing, collecting whether the buyer is a citizen or resident of a foreign adversary listed in 15 CFR 7.4, whether the land is within 10 miles of a military facility, intended use and other holdings; and bars foreign-adversary buyers within 10 miles of a staffed military facility. Its current status under the present governor was not checked.
- Iowa corporate farming: Code 9H.4(1)(c) exempts agricultural land (including leaseholds) acquired by a nonprofit corporation as defined in 9H.1(20)(a) (a corporation under section 504.141 that is not a foreign corporation).
- Missouri corporate farming: RSMo 350.015 (effective 28 Aug 2012) exempts any interest acquired by an educational, religious or charitable not-for-profit or pro forma corporation.
- Community land-trust precedent: Dancing Rabbit Land Trust holds the land and leases warrens (residential 0.01 USD per square foot per month, about 25 USD a month for a 2,500 sq ft leasehold, per Dancing Rabbit's cost-of-living page); Bear Creek leases for a lifetime or 99 years.
- Water permits: see water.md.

**Practitioner-relevant nuance:** Do not blur the two regimes: an Iowa parcel and a Missouri parcel a few kilometres apart carry different foreign-buyer rules, different corporate-farming exemptions and different county rules. County zoning, building and septic rules were not verified for any of the seven counties; Dancing Rabbit's own history says its founders searched in the 1990s for "no zoning, no building codes, affordable land", which is a historical account of a choice, not a present finding, and is also the displacement vector this framework names.
